# home-security-emergencies | Home Security During Emergencies
category: security
priority: normal
tags: home security, locks, lighting, evacuated home, looting prevention, safety
sources: ready-gov, fema, nfpa
related: guide:situational-awareness, guide:neighborhood-coordination, guide:fire-prevention-escape
summary: Simple, lawful steps to keep your home and family safe during outages, evacuations and unrest.

## Overview
Emergencies can increase the risk of theft and other crime, but the large majority of disaster-time hazards are accidents, fires and exposure. Focus on layered, low-conflict measures: deter, delay, and call authorities.

## At home
1. Keep doors and windows locked. Add deadbolts, door reinforcement and window pins.
2. Keep exterior lighting working with solar or battery lights. Light deters intruders and prevents falls.
3. Do not advertise that you have supplies, generators or that the house is empty. Keep generators quiet, secure and out of sight where possible.
4. Know your neighbors, and agree to watch each other's homes.
5. Keep your phone charged and a charged phone and whistle by the bed.
6. Plan how to call 911 if you cannot talk, and where to go safely inside your home.
7. Keep valuables and documents in a safe or out of sight.

## If you must leave
- Lock up, close windows and secure valuables.
- Tell trusted neighbors or family.
- Avoid posting travel plans publicly.
- Use timers or solar lights for the appearance of occupancy where it is safe.

## If you encounter a threat
Your safety is more important than property. Avoid confrontation. Get to a safe place, lock a door, and call 911. Provide the dispatcher your location and description. Follow their instructions.

## Lawful and safe approach
Know your local laws. Any equipment you keep for protection needs training, legal compliance and safe storage away from children. This app does not provide weapons instructions.

!! Do not use open flames, generators or stoves in closed rooms for security reasons. Fire and carbon monoxide are bigger risks than crime.

# situational-awareness | Situational Awareness
category: security
priority: normal
tags: awareness, observe, orient, safety habits, exits, crowds
sources: ready-gov, red-cross
related: guide:personal-safety, guide:home-security-emergencies
summary: Notice your surroundings, plan exits and trust your instincts to avoid trouble.

## Overview
Situational awareness means paying attention to what is happening around you and thinking about what could go wrong. It is a habit, not paranoia.

## Habits
1. When you arrive anywhere, note the exits, stairs and the way you came in.
2. Put your phone down while walking. Look up and around.
3. Pay attention to unusual behavior, crowds, smells, sounds (gas leak smell, smoke, alarms) and changes in the weather.
4. Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, leave.
5. Keep your keys, phone and essentials in a consistent pocket or bag.
6. In public events, identify the nearest exits and emergency staff.
7. In a hotel, learn the escape routes and count doors to the stairs.

## During emergencies
- Stay calm and breathe. Panic creates mistakes.
- Take in official information from sources you trust, and avoid rumors.
- Move away from danger early, and help those who need it without putting yourself at risk.
- Remain aware of weather alerts and changing conditions.

## Avoid risky situations
Do not walk or drive in floodwater or through areas with downed lines or damaged buildings. Avoid being alone in isolated areas at night. Tell someone where you are going.

>> Practice: each time you enter a new building, ask "if there was an emergency, how would I get out?"

# neighborhood-coordination | Neighborhood Coordination
category: security
priority: normal
tags: neighborhood watch, mutual aid, community, check-in, block captain
sources: ready-gov, fema
related: guide:neighborhood-preparedness, guide:community-resilience
summary: Organized, friendly neighbors reduce harm and crime and speed up help.

## Overview
Community coordination strengthens safety and recovery. It relies on trust and communication, not confrontation.

## Steps
1. Make a contact list (with permission) and a group chat or call tree for your street or building.
2. Pick a block captain or two and agree on roles: check-ins, supplies, first aid, communication.
3. Share which households need extra help (older adults, medical needs, young children, pets).
4. Agree on a visible signal for "OK" or "need help" in windows.
5. Meet at a set place after an event and conduct welfare checks in pairs.
6. Share information with local officials, and report hazards or crimes to 911 or the non-emergency line as appropriate.
7. Use your community's Neighborhood Watch or CERT resources, if available.

## Good practices
- Do not form armed patrols or confront suspected criminals. Observe and report.
- Share only verified information. Avoid spreading rumors.
- Respect privacy. Share personal details only with consent.
- Keep a spirit of mutual aid: share tools, power, water and skills.

!! Taking the law into your own hands creates danger for you and others. Report concerns to the authorities.

# fire-prevention-escape | Fire Prevention and Home Escape
category: security
priority: critical
tags: fire, smoke alarm, escape plan, extinguisher, stop drop roll, home fire
sources: nfpa, ready-gov, red-cross
related: checklist:shelter-in-place, guide:burns, guide:lighting-flashlights, guide:family-emergency-plan
summary: Working smoke alarms, a practiced escape plan and safe habits cut your risk dramatically.

## Overview
Home fires are the most common disaster in the U.S. Fires spread fast: you may have less than two minutes to escape. Smoke alarms and an escape plan save lives.

## Prevent fires
- Never leave cooking unattended. Keep flammable items away from the stove.
- Keep heaters, candles and fireplaces at least 3 feet from anything that can burn. Never leave them unattended or go to sleep with them on.
- Use LED lights instead of candles when you can.
- Use caution with generators, fuel and charging batteries. See other guides.
- Smoking: use deep, sturdy ashtrays and never smoke in bed or near oxygen.
- Do not overload outlets, and replace damaged cords.
- Store matches and lighters out of reach of children.

## Smoke alarms
1. Install on every level, inside every bedroom and outside each sleeping area.
2. Test monthly and replace batteries as directed. Replace alarms about every 10 years.
3. Interconnected alarms sound everywhere in the house.
4. Make sure everyone can hear them, and consider special alarms for people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

## Escape plan
1. Draw a floor plan showing **two ways out of every room**.
2. Choose an outside meeting place.
3. Practice at least twice a year, including at night.
4. Make sure doors and windows open easily and are not blocked.
5. Teach children to leave and not to hide or go back.

## If there is a fire
1. **Get out, stay out, and call 911** from outside.
2. If there is smoke, stay low where the air is cleaner.
3. Feel closed doors with the back of your hand. If it is hot, use another exit.
4. Close doors behind you to slow fire spread.
5. If your clothes catch fire: stop, drop and roll.
6. If trapped, close doors, seal gaps with towels, signal at a window, and call 911.

## Extinguishers
Keep an ABC extinguisher in the kitchen and garage. Use the PASS method: Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep. Fight only a small fire when you have an exit at your back.

!! Never go back into a burning building for belongings or pets. Tell firefighters.

# personal-safety | Basic Personal Safety
category: security
priority: normal
tags: personal safety, avoiding danger, travel, walking, awareness, check in
sources: ready-gov, red-cross
related: guide:situational-awareness, guide:home-security-emergencies
summary: Practical habits to lower risk when travelling, waiting in lines or moving through disrupted areas.

## Overview
After a disaster, normal systems fail: traffic signals, lighting, services. Basic personal safety is about prevention and calm decisions.

## Habits
1. Travel in groups when possible, in daylight and on known routes.
2. Tell someone your plan and when to expect you.
3. Keep valuables out of sight. Carry limited cash, and split it.
4. Keep your phone charged and keep a whistle, flashlight and ID on you.
5. Avoid areas with damaged buildings, downed lines, flooding, fires or unrest.
6. Do not accept rides or help from strangers if you feel uncomfortable. Use official shelters and volunteers.
7. Use well-lit, populated places. Park in lit areas, and check your back seat before entering your car.
8. Do not draw attention to supplies, fuel or generators.

## Avoiding dangerous areas
- Follow road closures and official instructions.
- Leave areas where crowds are tense or violent.
- Do not enter flooded roads or damaged structures.
- Look for exits in crowded settings and stay near the edges.

## If you feel threatened
Move to a safe, public place and call 911. Make noise to attract attention. Give up valuables if demanded. Your safety is more important than property.

## Self-defense
Consider a certified class in situational awareness or self-defense. Know local laws. This app does not promote violence or vigilantism.

>> Keep a copy of your emergency contact card in your wallet and phone lock-screen.

# vulnerable-members | Protecting Vulnerable Household Members
category: security
priority: high
tags: children, elderly, disabilities, vulnerable, supervision, safeguarding, abuse prevention
sources: ready-gov, red-cross, fema
related: guide:children-preparedness, guide:elderly-preparedness, guide:disabilities-preparedness
summary: Children, older adults and people with disabilities need extra supervision, support and safeguarding in emergencies.

## Overview
Emergencies disrupt routines, supervision and services. Vulnerable people may be at higher risk of injury, exploitation, abuse or neglect, and of being separated.

## Steps
1. Keep children close and in sight, especially in shelters, crowds and evacuations. Never leave them alone in vehicles or unsupervised in unfamiliar places.
2. Give each child an ID card or wristband with your contact information and a recent photo on your phone.
3. Teach children to approach uniformed staff or a trusted adult if separated, and not to go with strangers.
4. Use the buddy system in shelters and know who is around your family. Use staff-supervised areas for children when available.
5. For older adults and people with disabilities, keep medications, mobility aids and communication tools within reach, and keep a trusted caregiver nearby.
6. Watch for signs of distress, neglect or mistreatment, and report concerns to shelter staff or authorities.
7. Maintain privacy and dignity: provide private places for changing and medical needs where possible.
8. Plan for reunification if you are separated and register with the Red Cross or official tracing services.

## In shelters
Ask for the manager, volunteers or law enforcement if you feel unsafe. Request reasonable accommodations and accessible spaces. Keep valuables and documents close.

## Mental health
Provide reassurance and routines. Seek professional help for severe or lasting distress.

!! Report suspected abuse or exploitation to shelter staff, law enforcement or the appropriate hotline.

# privacy-documents | Emergency Privacy and Document Security
category: security
priority: normal
tags: privacy, identity theft, document security, passwords, encryption, backup
sources: ready-gov, fema
related: guide:emergency-documents, guide:medical-records
summary: Protect sensitive information stored in kits and on phones so a lost bag or phone does not become a second disaster.

## Overview
Go-bags, phones and backups hold sensitive information. Protect it without making it unavailable when you need it.

## Practices
1. Keep paper copies of documents in a locked, waterproof container or a bag you control at all times.
2. Black out or omit full account and Social Security numbers on copies where possible. Store a separate list of full numbers securely.
3. Encrypt digital copies. Use a strong passcode and a reputable password manager. Do not keep passwords written on the same device.
4. Use a passcode and biometrics on your phone, and enable "Find my phone" features and remote wipe.
5. Do not post evacuation plans, home absence, addresses or documents on social media.
6. Back up data offline, such as an encrypted USB drive or the export function in this app, and keep backups in separate places.
7. Beware of scams after disasters: fraudulent relief offers, fake charities and contractors. Verify identities, never give payment by gift card, and use official sites.
8. Shred documents you no longer need.

## This app
Cairn stores household information only on your device. It does not transmit data to any service. If you export a backup, you control where it goes. Protect the file because it may contain medical and contact details.

!! If your phone with sensitive information is lost, change passwords, report it to your carrier and use remote lock or erase.
