# pandemic-outbreak | Pandemics and Disease Outbreaks
category: threats
priority: high
tags: pandemic, outbreak, epidemic, infectious disease, quarantine, isolation, masks, vaccines
sources: cdc, ready-gov, red-cross
related: guide:infection-prevention-hygiene, guide:handwashing-hygiene, guide:prepare-two-weeks, guide:medication-storage-continuity, guide:extended-disruptions
summary: Reduce spread with hygiene, staying home when sick and following public health advice. Prepare supplies and plans for several weeks of disruption.

## Overview
An outbreak is a rise in cases of an infectious disease. A pandemic spreads across regions or the world. Effects can include illness in your household, closed schools and workplaces, strained health care and disrupted supply chains. Public health advice changes as an outbreak develops, so rely on current guidance from your health department and the CDC.

## Preparing beforehand
1. Keep vaccinations up to date and talk to your clinician about those recommended for your household.
2. Build a supply of food, water, hygiene items and a thermometer for at least two weeks (see Two Weeks guide).
3. Keep at least a month of essential prescription medications if your prescriber and insurer allow, and over-the-counter items you use.
4. Make a plan for childcare, school closure, work, caring for a sick family member and caring for pets.
5. Identify a separate room and bathroom you could use for someone who is sick.
6. Learn how to reach your local health department and a nurse line.

## During an outbreak
- **Stay home when sick** and keep sick people away from others where possible.
- **Wash hands** with soap and water for at least 20 seconds. Use alcohol-based sanitizer (at least 60% alcohol) when you cannot wash.
- Improve **ventilation**: open windows when practical, and use air filters.
- Wear a **well-fitting mask** when public health officials advise it, when you are sick and around others, or when caring for someone sick.
- Clean frequently touched surfaces.
- Follow guidance on testing, isolation and quarantine, and treatments.
- Take care of mental health: keep routines, stay connected remotely and seek help when stressed.

## Caring for someone at home
Give fluids and rest, monitor symptoms, keep them in a separate space, use masks and handwashing, and do not share towels or dishes.

## Seek emergency care for
Trouble breathing, persistent chest pain or pressure, new confusion, being unable to wake or stay awake, pale, gray or blue-tinted lips or skin, or any symptom that is severe or rapidly worsening. Call 911 and say you suspect an infectious illness.

## Common mistakes
- Waiting too long to get help for breathing difficulty.
- Relying on social media instead of public health sources.
- Hoarding supplies and leaving others without.

!! Infants, older adults, pregnant people and those with weakened immune systems are at higher risk from many infections. Call a clinician early.

# active-violence | Active Shooter and Violent Attacks
category: threats
priority: critical
tags: active shooter, violence, run hide fight, attack, lockdown, mass casualty, police response
sources: dhs, ready-gov, red-cross
related: guide:situational-awareness, guide:bleeding-control, guide:personal-safety, guide:children-preparedness
summary: Run if you can, hide if you cannot, and fight only as a last resort to survive. Call 911 when it is safe and obey police on arrival.

## Overview
Attacks with firearms and other weapons are rare, fast and unpredictable. Public safety agencies teach three options: **Run, Hide, Fight.** Your goal is to get away from the danger, protect yourself and help responders do their job.

## Be prepared
1. When you enter a building, note exits, stairs and places to hide.
2. Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, leave.
3. Know your workplace, school and venue emergency plans and take part in drills.
4. Learn bleeding control and CPR (see those guides).
5. Teach children simple rules: listen to adults, stay quiet and follow school plans.

## RUN
- If there is a safe path, **leave immediately**, even if others hesitate.
- Leave your belongings. Help others if you can but do not wait for them.
- Keep your hands visible and empty as you approach police.
- Once safe, call 911.

## HIDE
- Get out of the attacker's view and sight lines. Go behind a solid wall or object.
- Lock or barricade the door with heavy furniture. Turn off lights, silence your phone (including vibration) and stay low and quiet.
- Do not open the door until you are certain it is police. They will identify themselves clearly.
- If you can call 911 safely, say where you are, how many attackers, descriptions and weapons.

## FIGHT
- As a **last resort**, if your life is in immediate danger, act with commitment and aggression. Use anything you can as a weapon, work with others and aim to disable the attacker.

## When police arrive
- Remain calm and follow instructions. Keep hands up, spread fingers and visible. Do not run toward officers, point, scream or hold anything.
- Officers may not stop to help the injured. They will go to the threat first. Medical help comes after the area is secure.
- Leave the way officers direct and follow their orders.

## Aftermath
Seek medical care for injuries. Expect strong emotions and seek counseling. Call or text 988 if you or someone else is in crisis.

!! Your priority is survival. These steps are for self-protection. This guide does not teach confrontation or armed response.

# explosions-suspicious-items | Explosions, Bombings and Suspicious Items
category: threats
priority: high
tags: explosion, bomb, suspicious package, suspicious item, terrorism, blast, secondary device, unattended bag
sources: dhs, ready-gov, red-cross
related: guide:active-violence, guide:bleeding-control, guide:structural-collapse, guide:burns, guide:situational-awareness
summary: Move away from suspicious items without touching them, and if an explosion occurs, leave the area, avoid hazards and give first aid only when safe.

## Suspicious items
Signs include an unattended bag or package in an unusual place, wires or batteries visible, strange smells or stains, or someone acting suspiciously around it. Remember "If you see something, say something".
1. **Do not touch, move or open it.**
2. Move away and keep others away. Go as far as you can and put solid objects between you and the item.
3. Report it to security or call 911. Describe it and its location.
4. Follow instructions from police. Do not use radios or phones right next to the item.

## If there is an explosion
1. **Take cover** under a sturdy table or against an interior wall away from windows. Protect your head.
2. When safe, **leave the building** quickly using stairs, not elevators. Watch for falling debris, broken glass, fire and weakened floors.
3. Stay low under smoke and cover your nose and mouth with cloth.
4. Move well away from the scene, upwind. **Secondary devices** or collapses may occur. Do not go back.
5. Call 911 when safe. Give your location and what you saw.
6. Help others only if it is safe for you to do so.

## First aid
Treat life-threatening bleeding first (see Bleeding Control), then burns and breathing problems. Blast injuries can be internal and hidden: anyone near a blast needs medical evaluation even if they feel fine. Ringing in the ears, chest pain, trouble breathing, abdominal pain and confusion are warning signs.

## Afterward
Follow instructions from officials. Leave the area clear for emergency vehicles. Reunite with family by using text and your out-of-area contact. Seek help for emotional distress.

## Common mistakes
- Picking up or moving a package.
- Staying to film or watch.
- Returning for belongings.
- Blocking roads that responders need.

!! Do not try to examine or disarm a suspicious item yourself.

# cyber-grid-attack | Cyberattacks and Infrastructure Disruptions
category: threats
priority: normal
tags: cyberattack, ransomware, grid failure, internet outage, bank outage, infrastructure, disinformation, scams
sources: dhs, ready-gov, fema
related: guide:extended-disruptions, guide:extended-power-outages, guide:emergency-budgeting, guide:privacy-documents, guide:comms-network-failure
summary: Attacks on networks, power, water or banks can interrupt daily life. Prepare with cash, supplies, paper copies, offline information and good digital habits.

## Overview
A cyberattack can disrupt payment systems, internet and phone service, hospitals, water treatment, fuel pipelines or the power grid. Most are limited and repaired within hours or days, but some last longer. Preparing for them resembles preparing for any extended outage, with added attention to money, information and scams.

## Preparing beforehand
1. Keep **cash in small bills** at home. Card readers and ATMs may be down.
2. Keep two weeks of food, water, medications and fuel in your vehicle (at least half a tank).
3. Keep **paper copies** of key numbers, medical information, account details and your family plan, and an offline copy of this app.
4. Own a battery or crank radio and know your local emergency radio stations.
5. Keep devices updated, use strong unique passwords and a password manager, turn on multi-factor authentication and back up important files offline.
6. Know your bank's emergency contacts.

## During a disruption
- Check official sources (radio, local government, utility) rather than social media rumors.
- Conserve phone battery. Use text messages. Use your family plan and out-of-area contact.
- Conserve water and fuel. Fuel pumps and stores may not work.
- Be alert to **scams** that exploit confusion: unexpected calls, texts and emails asking for money, passwords or codes. Do not click unknown links.
- If you think your device is infected (ransomware), disconnect it from the network, do not pay without advice and report it to authorities.
- Check on neighbors, especially people who rely on power, internet or medical devices.

## Afterward
Check bank statements and credit reports, change passwords, update software and restock supplies. Note what you needed, and adjust your plan.

## Common mistakes
- Relying only on one payment method.
- Sharing unverified information.
- Hoarding fuel unsafely.

!! Never store fuel unsafely or beyond local limits. See Fuel Storage Safety.

# solar-storm-emp | Solar Storms and Electromagnetic Pulses
category: threats
priority: normal
tags: solar storm, geomagnetic storm, EMP, space weather, power grid, GPS, radio, blackout
sources: noaa, dhs, fema
related: guide:extended-power-outages, guide:weather-radio, guide:offline-maps-navigation, guide:comms-network-failure, guide:extended-disruptions
summary: Severe space weather or an electromagnetic pulse could disrupt power, radio and GPS. The right preparation is practical outage readiness.

## Overview
**Geomagnetic storms** happen when the Sun ejects charged particles that disturb Earth's magnetic field. NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center rates them from G1 (minor) to G5 (extreme). Strong storms can disturb the power grid, satellite navigation, high-frequency radio and satellites. They are not directly harmful to people on the ground.
An **electromagnetic pulse (EMP)** is a burst of electromagnetic energy, for example from a high-altitude nuclear explosion. This is considered unlikely, and effects depend on the type, location and size. Claims about EMPs are often exaggerated, so rely on official sources.

## Preparing beforehand
Plan as for a long power outage:
1. Keep a battery or crank radio, spare batteries and a way to receive alerts.
2. Keep two weeks of water, food and medications, and a way to cook and stay warm or cool safely.
3. Keep paper maps, a compass and a printed contact list. GPS and mobile networks may be unreliable.
4. Keep cash, fuel in your vehicle and an out-of-area contact.
5. Keep important documents on paper.
6. Consider a power bank and power station, and keep spare radios and chargers unplugged and stored.

## During an event
- Follow NOAA space weather alerts and official instructions.
- Expect the possibility of power outages, radio and GPS interference. Use landlines or text if available.
- If the grid fails, follow the Extended Power Outages guide, including food safety and carbon monoxide safety.
- Unplug sensitive equipment if an outage is expected and use surge protection as advised.

## Afterward
Restore equipment gradually when power returns. Check food in the refrigerator. Restock supplies.

## Common mistakes
- Spending heavily on products that make exaggerated EMP claims.
- Assuming devices that were off or unplugged will always survive. Results are uncertain.
- Ignoring the basics: water, food, warmth, communication.

!! Never use generators, grills or camp stoves indoors during a long outage.

# civil-unrest | Civil Unrest and Large Crowds
category: threats
priority: normal
tags: civil unrest, riot, protest, crowd, curfew, looting, stay safe, crowd crush
sources: ready-gov, red-cross, dhs
related: guide:personal-safety, guide:situational-awareness, guide:home-security-emergencies, guide:evacuation-planning, guide:neighborhood-coordination
summary: Stay away from unrest, keep your household safe at home, and know how to get out of a crowd without getting hurt.

## Overview
Civil unrest includes riots, large protests that turn violent, looting and curfews. Most people are never directly involved, but it can disrupt travel, stores, services and safety. The aim is to avoid risk, stay informed and stay calm.

## Preparing beforehand
1. Keep supplies for at least 3 days at home so you do not need to go out.
2. Keep your vehicle fueled and your go-bag packed.
3. Know alternative routes and several ways to reach home and work.
4. Agree on a family plan: where each person will go, how you will check in and your meeting place.
5. Keep phones charged and a battery radio.

## If unrest is nearby
- **Stay away.** Avoid the area, including streets that lead to it.
- Stay home. Lock doors and windows, keep lights low and keep a low profile. Do not post your location or what you have.
- Follow curfews and instructions from authorities.
- Move children away from windows. Keep pets inside.
- Do not confront rioters or looters. Call 911 for immediate danger and give descriptions.
- Do not drive toward the unrest. If you encounter a crowd or blocked road, calmly turn around and leave.

## If you are caught in a crowd
1. Move toward the edges and exits early. Do not move toward the center.
2. Avoid walls, fences, glass and narrow spaces, where you can be trapped.
3. Keep your arms up in front of your chest to protect your breathing space.
4. If you fall, protect your head, curl up and get to your feet as soon as possible.
5. If police use crowd control measures such as tear gas, move away, upwind. Cover your nose and mouth, do not rub your eyes and flush them with clean water afterward.

## Afterward
Check on neighbors. Document damage for insurance. Be careful of rumors and follow official reports.

!! Your safety is more important than property. Do not use force to protect belongings.

# structural-collapse | Building Collapse and Being Trapped
category: threats
priority: high
tags: collapse, trapped, rubble, earthquake, explosion, building collapse, rescue, crush injury
sources: ready-gov, fema, red-cross, usgs
related: guide:earthquakes, guide:explosions-suspicious-items, guide:bleeding-control, guide:returning-home, guide:gas-leak
summary: If trapped, protect your airway, make noise without shouting and conserve energy. If you are a rescuer, call for professionals and do not enter unstable structures.

## If you are trapped
1. Stay calm. Take slow breaths and check yourself for injuries. Control bleeding with pressure.
2. **Do not light a match or lighter.** Gas may be present.
3. **Cover your mouth and nose** with cloth to avoid breathing dust.
4. **Tap on a pipe or wall**, or use a whistle or phone to signal. Shout only as a last resort, because it makes you inhale dust.
5. If you have a phone, text or call 911. Texts may go through when calls cannot. Turn on a flashlight to be found.
6. Do not move around or kick up dust. Remain still if the space is unstable.
7. Conserve phone battery and energy. Use any water carefully.
8. Tell rescuers about injuries when found. Do not try to climb out of rubble on your own if it seems unstable.

## If you are a bystander
1. Move away from the building. Further collapse is possible.
2. **Call 911.** Give the location and number of people believed to be trapped.
3. Do not enter unstable buildings or go into rubble unless you are trained and it is safe. Untrained rescuers often become victims.
4. Listen for calls and signal sounds, and tell responders where you hear people.
5. Give first aid to people who are out and in a safe place.

## Crush injuries
People trapped under heavy weight for hours can become seriously ill even when they appear fine once freed. Any person pinned for a long time needs urgent medical care. Do not give them food or drink unless advised by medical staff.

## After rescue
Seek medical care for any injuries, smoke inhalation and dust exposure. Avoid the area. Expect strong emotions and seek support.

!! Gas leaks, fires and aftershocks make collapsed buildings dangerous. Leave rescue to trained teams.

# avalanches | Avalanches
category: threats
priority: normal
tags: avalanche, backcountry, snow, beacon, probe, shovel, mountain, skiing
sources: usfs, nws, red-cross
related: guide:winter-storms, guide:hypothermia, guide:cpr-aed, guide:landslides
summary: Avalanches are fast and deadly. Avoid avalanche terrain in dangerous conditions, carry rescue gear and training, and act quickly if someone is buried.

## Understanding the threat
Avalanches happen when a layer of snow breaks loose and slides down a slope. Most victims are recreationists who trigger the slide themselves. Burial can kill in minutes. Roads and homes in mountain areas can also be hit.

## Preparing beforehand
1. **Check the avalanche forecast** from your regional avalanche center and heed advisories.
2. Take an avalanche safety course before entering backcountry terrain.
3. Carry a **beacon, probe and shovel** and know how to use them. Travel with partners, not alone.
4. Avoid slopes of about 30 to 45 degrees in dangerous conditions, and avoid terrain traps such as gullies.
5. Residents of avalanche zones should follow local evacuation orders and road closures.

## Warning signs
Recent heavy snowfall, rapid warming, strong wind loading, cracking or "whumpfing" sounds in the snow, and recent avalanches.

## If caught
1. Try to escape to the side. Let go of poles and gear.
2. Try to grab trees or rocks. Make swimming motions to stay near the surface.
3. As the slide slows, put a hand in front of your face to make an air pocket and try to push an arm toward the surface.
4. Stay calm and conserve air.

## If someone is buried
1. Watch where they disappear.
2. **Switch beacons to search** and start looking at once. Survival drops sharply after about 15 minutes.
3. Probe and dig. Call 911 as soon as you can, but do not leave the victim to do so if you are alone.
4. Clear the airway, give first aid and treat for hypothermia. Start CPR if needed.

## Common mistakes
- Going out without gear or training.
- Ignoring warnings.
- Spreading out too little or too much on a slope: cross one at a time.

!! This guide is not a substitute for avalanche training. Courses are essential.

# dam-levee-failure | Dam and Levee Failure
category: threats
priority: normal
tags: dam failure, levee, dam break, inundation, flood wave, evacuation zone, flooding
sources: fema, nws, ready-gov
related: guide:flooding, guide:flash-floods, guide:evacuation-planning, guide:emergency-documents
summary: A dam or levee failure can cause sudden, severe flooding. Know whether you are in an inundation zone and leave to high ground immediately when warned.

## Understanding the threat
Dams and levees protect land but can fail through heavy rain, overtopping, erosion, earthquakes or poor maintenance. Failure can release a fast, deep, debris-filled flood with little warning. People downstream of a dam are at risk even if it is miles away.

## Preparing beforehand
1. Find out whether you live, work or travel through a **dam or levee inundation zone**. Ask your local emergency management office for maps.
2. Learn the warning system: sirens, alerts and phone notifications.
3. Know evacuation routes and where high ground is. Plan routes on foot as well as by car.
4. Buy flood insurance if at risk. Levee-protected areas are still flood-prone.
5. Keep a go-bag, documents above ground and a family plan.

## Warning signs
Official alerts, rising reservoir levels, unusual leaks or muddy water, sinkholes or cracks in a dam, or a roaring sound upstream.

## During the event
1. **Evacuate immediately** when told. Do not wait.
2. Move to high ground, away from the river or valley. Go sideways to the flood path, not along it.
3. Do not drive into floodwater and do not cross bridges over flowing water.
4. If trapped, go to the highest point and signal for help. Call 911.

## Afterward
Follow the guidance for flood recovery: avoid floodwater, use safe water, check for hazards and clean up safely. Do not return until officials clear it.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming a nearby levee makes flooding impossible.
- Trying to outrun water by car.
- Waiting for a second warning.

!! Treat any warning of dam failure as a flash flood emergency.

# sinkholes | Sinkholes
category: threats
priority: normal
tags: sinkhole, ground collapse, karst, cracks, subsidence, land
sources: usgs, fema, ready-gov
related: guide:landslides, guide:gas-leak, guide:returning-home, guide:earthquakes
summary: Sinkholes can open suddenly. Know the warning signs, keep away from the edge and call for help.

## Understanding the threat
A sinkhole is a depression or hole that forms when the ground collapses, usually where water dissolves limestone or other rock, or where pipes leak and wash away soil. Some open suddenly, while others appear slowly. They are most common in areas such as Florida, Texas, Alabama, Missouri, Kentucky, Tennessee and Pennsylvania.

## Warning signs
New cracks in the ground, walls, floors or foundations, doors and windows that stick, depressions or circular dips in the yard, sagging fences or leaning trees, muddy well water, small pits or water pooling in new places, and exposed tree roots or foundations.

## Preparing beforehand
- Check geological maps and local information if you live in sinkhole-prone areas.
- Keep drains and gutters directing water away from the home. Repair leaking pipes.
- Consider insurance: sinkhole coverage is often excluded from standard policies.

## If a sinkhole opens
1. **Keep away from the edge.** Edges can collapse. Keep children and pets away.
2. If you are inside and the ground is moving, leave quickly if safe to do so.
3. Call 911 if anyone has fallen in or may be at risk. Do not go in to rescue.
4. Call the utilities if you suspect a broken gas, water, sewer or electrical line. If you smell gas, see the Gas Leak guide.
5. Warn neighbors. Mark off the area with tape or cones from a safe distance.
6. Contact local officials and an engineer or geologist before using the area.

## Afterward
Do not return to a damaged building until it is cleared. Photograph the damage and contact your insurance company. Do not fill the hole yourself, since improper filling can make it worse.

## Common mistakes
- Walking to the edge to look.
- Dumping material in the hole.
- Ignoring early cracks.

!! If you are driving and see a hole or a collapsed road, stop and turn around. Call 911.
